1 · How high can we aim?  ·  Challenge A: attendee growthMd Misbah Ur Rahman · RISA Labs APM (Growth) take-home

Aim: 3,440 verified visitors in Pune — 1.7× the 2025 edition — on the same ₹3.0 lakh. Commit: 2,850.

CampusFest 2027, Sat–Sun 6–7 March. Metric: distinct external people with an admitted, verified check-in. 2025: 3,625 identified → 2,800 registered → 2,500 confirmed → 1,925 came + 125 walk-ins = 2,050 visitors (goal 2,200). Leaks: 825 never registered (275 abandoned the form), 88 stuck waitlisted, 575 confirmed never got in — 60 of them lost at the gate, all Campus-Ambassador registrants.

Why Pune

  • S517 colleges, 3rd-most of any district, after Bengaluru Urban 1,148 and Jaipur 742 (AISHE 2023-24); 13.9 lakh aged 18–24, 8.7 lakh urban (Census 2011).
  • SThe 16 largest institutions teach ≈1.4 lakh students (NIRF returns).
  • SMetro: 3 lines, 30% student pass, interchange at Shivajinagar.
  • SEarly March: before SPPU exams (23 Mar) and April heat (35.6 vs 38.1°C, IMD).
  • Local edge: I live in Pune; club and venue work is done in person.

Sanity checks and the binding constraint

  • Stress tests (base 2,854): peer β 0.5 → 2,536; scaled channels' show-up halfway to the 2025 average → 2,729; reserve unreleased → 2,670; all three → 2,322, still above 2,050. β is tested first (week 3).
  • Market: 3,441 = 0.4% of Pune's urban 18–24. Comparables: ≈4,700 entries over two days vs the 6k–40k "footfall" Pune fests claim.
  • Capacity — the gate binds, not the ground: peak ~420 admissions/hour vs 251 (bet 3); ~2,400 on the busier day vs an 8–10k event police cleared at a Shivajinagar ground in 2026. Apply early for police permission.

2 · Where will you place your bets?

Five bets, ranked: build on groups and friends, stop losing people after they say yes — funded by cutting the two channels whose people didn't come.

Ranking = expected extra verified visitors × confidence in the evidence ÷ cost (₹ + team time); ties broken by how fast we learn. Contributions come from the target model, applied in rank order so they add up (base / stretch).

Double down

Bets 1 and 2 each hold back ₹12.5k (a ₹25k reserve) until week 4 and get it only if their scale line holds; an unearned share goes to the next bet past its line. They are the cheapest visitors we have and the most uncertain at scale, so we pay to find out early.

Deprioritise or reject

  • Creator Partners as run: ₹337 per visitor, 60% show-up, most opt-outs after paid. Kept only as trackable codes.
  • Paid tickets or deposits: within the same event, payers show up only 2–4 points more (PheedLoop, 195 events) — not worth the registrations lost.
  • A big-name headliner: a large share of ₹3L for an unmeasured effect, plus permission and crowd risk.
  • More cold paid reach: it was saturating within the 2025 campaign.

Dependencies, overlap, risks

  • Overlap: ambassadors and partnerships share campuses → split the college list; one recorded source per person, rewards on verified attendance only. Bet 3 runs the registration check; bet 5 pays only for registrations that pass it.
  • Dependency: referrals need a registrant base — bets 1 and 5 feed bet 2.
  • Capacity: success raises gate load to ~420/hour → bet 3 is a precondition, not an extra.
  • Permission: NH7 Weekender 2024 was cancelled when police refused permission → apply first.

3 · How will we know what's working?

Attendance shows only on the day — so each bet is judged weekly on leading indicators, against lines set in advance.

Two rules: judge rates only on cohorts at least 7 days old (in 2025, 95% of confirmations came within 4.1 days — younger cohorts always look worse), and keep a small holdout wherever we can, so next year's data can answer what 2025's could not.

Cost = cost per registration ÷ the all-campaign blended cost in the same period, so expensive early weeks don't trip the lines; bets stop at 1.3×, tighter than the 1.6× campaign default, because they carry extra money. Attendance forecast for 2027: Σ confirmed registrants per channel × that channel's 2025 show-up (channel mix alone moved 2025 show-up between 59% and 92%). The 2025 replay has no earlier year, so the product projects registration pace × the plan's visitors-per-registration; backtested on 1 Mar, 15 Mar and 1 Apr it was off by −1%, −15% and +3%, so triggers need two readings in a row.

When performance changes — the playbook, decided in advance

  • A bet passes its scale line: at week 4 bets 1 and 2 get their held-back ₹12.5k; a share not earned goes to the next bet past its line (1 → 2 → 4). Nothing is released earlier.
  • A bet hits its stop line: its remaining budget moves in a fixed order — referrals, then partnerships, then organic listings. Never back to cold paid reach.
  • A rate turns bad on a mature cohort: "change" first (incentive, message, audience), re-read one week later, stop only if it stays below the line.
  • Projected visitors below 2,850 two weeks running, from three weeks out: turn on the stretch levers early — calls to non-responders, waitlist release, more group passes.
  • Gate dry run below 450 admissions/hour a week out: add lanes and scanners, and stagger entry using reconfirmation data.
  • Seen in the 2025 replay: every projection from 15 March on was below the 3,200 goal (2,368 on 15 Mar, needing 60.7/day vs 29.9; 2,882 on 1 Apr; actual 2,800) — the shortfall was visible four weeks out, though single readings swung by up to 15%.

Weekly rhythm

  • Monday: the Growth Center flags every campaign against its lines; bet owners report their KPIs against the lines above.
  • Owners act within 48 hours; each action is a task with an owner and due date.
  • Week 4: reserve released only to bets past their scale line.

How the dashboard and AI help

  • Dashboard: any date can be replayed; funnel vs the plan in force; where people drop out; decisions with their evidence; a budget what-if using this same model.
  • AI copilot: answers only from live data through the same metric functions, shows its steps, says when the data can't answer (e.g. location, causal email effect), and turns a recommendation into a task only after you confirm.
  • Tested: every metric is computed twice, in SQL and separately in pandas (2,050 values, 0 mismatches). The copilot passed all 38 automatic checks on 64 questions written by a separate AI agent that never saw its code, and declined all 8 it should — about ₹0.3 per answer.
